37. **The power-aware gate criterion (spec A7) is recorded as a reference proposal before the fix-round re-run, not written after it.** Task 5 of the fix-round plan; docs only, no code or test touched. This descends from the user's option (b1) in `docs/gate-0-1-report.md` §10, and §10 states its own condition: a criterion written after seeing the data "is the shape of p-hacking even when the logic is sound — so it must be written down *before* the re-run, not after." A7 therefore lands as its own commit, ahead of Task 6, titled **"reference proposal (recorded 2026-09-05, before the fix-round re-run)"**. User decision on framing: the thresholds below are a **reference proposal written before the fix-round results, not the binding criterion** — A7 does not amend the §3 gate 0→1 line. The final thresholds will be set by the user after reviewing the fix-round outputs (report §11); any difference from this reference is recorded there with its rationale, so the reader can see what was proposed blind and what was chosen informed. What the blind record buys is therefore not an unmovable bar but an auditable one: the delta between proposed and chosen is itself the evidence.

    **Proposed thresholds (recorded blind).** G1a, live anomalies (`Mom12m`, `ShareIss1Y`): Pearson ρ ≥ **0.85** against OSAP `deciles_ew` LS over the maximal two-source window **and** `mean_ours` within **[0.5×, 1.5×]** of `mean_osap` — both conditions, both anomalies. G1b, dormant anomalies (`EarningsSurprise`, `Accruals`): `|mean_ours − mean_osap|` ≤ **0.5%/month**, ρ reported but not gated. This is a **tightening** in substance despite relaxing the letter of "ρ > 0.9 on 3 of 4": it demands both live anomalies reproduce in level as well as in shape, where the old rule could be cleared by two dormant series whose ρ measures panel composition. Ruling 31's investable-universe screen (`universe_fn=universe.build`) is the panel, unchanged.

    **Live/dormant classification, computed once from OSAP's own published series before looking at ours** — `data/raw/osap/<signal>.csv`, 2016-01-29..2019-12-31, n = 48 monthly decimal returns each, t = mean / (sd/√n) with ddof = 1: `Mom12m` mean +0.840%/mo, sd 5.685%, **t = +1.02**; `ShareIss1Y` +1.310%, 3.901%, **t = +2.33**; `EarningsSurprise` −0.395%, 1.324%, **t = −2.07**; `Accruals` +0.026%, 1.622%, **t = +0.11**. These reproduce the gate report's §4.4 table exactly, so ruling 36's power argument and A7's classification rest on the same four numbers.

    **Classifier correction, ledgered rather than quietly applied.** The plan's draft of A7 phrased the classifier as "|t| ≥ 2 over the window ⇒ live" while naming `Mom12m` and `ShareIss1Y` as the live pair. On the numbers above those two statements disagree: |t| ≥ 2 would classify `EarningsSurprise` (−2.07) live and `Mom12m` (+1.02) dormant. The classifier as proposed is report §10 (b1)'s original rule — **live ⇔ |mean_osap| > 0.5%/month** — which does produce the named pair. The substantive reason it is the right rule, not merely the convenient one: G1a tests whether we reproduce a spread that exists, and `EarningsSurprise` is significant here only *inverted versus the literature* (ruling 36), so gating ρ on it would gate on reproducing a sign flip; while `Mom12m` pays a real +0.84%/month that 48 months cannot certify, and it is precisely the anomaly the fix round's dividend and delisting work should move, so low power must not excuse it out of the gate. 39. **`metrics.monthly_longshort` books dividend income and delisting exits; `backtest/engine.py` is deliberately untouched.** Tasks 3–4 of the fix round, ledger event `ac1276f4d3fa4500a7aea4eaf1acf3b2` (`decision.metrics.returns`). This changes a measured number: every calibration ρ and every `mean_ours` in report §11 is computed on a different series than ruling 36's. The two rules, v0, verbatim:

    > **Dividends.** A name's hold return is `(p1 + D) / p0 - 1`, where `D` is the cash per share of every dividend whose ex-date falls in `(formed, held_to]`. That window is half-open deliberately: the holder of record at the close *before* the ex-date is paid, and this portfolio is bought at the formation close, so a dividend going ex on the formation date belongs to the previous holder while one going ex on the hold end is ours. The short leg pays away what the long leg receives, and `long - short` books that for free. Rates come from `warehouse.actions.read_dividends`, already divided onto the store's split-adjusted price basis (spec A3).

    > **Delistings.** A name in a leg with no vetted close at `held_to`, but whose **last canonical close in the whole loaded panel** falls strictly inside `(formed, held_to)`, has been delisted mid-hold. It exits at that last close. If that close is below `DELIST_PRICE_FLOOR` (= $1.0) a further `DELIST_RETURN` (= −0.30, Shumway 1997's average for performance delistings whose final return is missing) is applied on top, because a name forced off an exchange under a dollar typically loses most of the residual value in the OTC aftermarket. A name whose last close is on or after `held_to` but missing *at* `held_to` is a quarantined or skipped day, not a delisting, and is still dropped.

    Why the lower bound is strict: a name whose last close *is* the formation close carries no evidence of what it was worth afterwards, so booking an exit there would assert a 0% hold return — a claim, not a measurement. The upper bound is strict for the mirror reason: a later print is proof the name survived the hold. (The two bounds meet without a gap — a name whose last close is exactly `held_to` is priced at `held_to` and never reaches the exit path — so the literal mutation `< held_to` → `<= held_to` is semantically inert; the bound that is actually load-bearing is *having* an upper bound at all, and that is what the gap test guards.)

    **Expected direction of the change.** Both delisting channels previously biased the spread *down*: a performance delisting concentrates in the **short** leg, and dropping a −80% name raised that leg's mean; an acquisition premium concentrates among **winners**, and dropping it lowered the long leg's mean. So the fix should *widen* our spreads, which is the direction A7's `mean_ours ∈ [0.5×, 1.5×] mean_osap` condition needs — stated here in advance so that a re-run which widens them is confirmation rather than a result read backwards.

    **Two residual imprecisions, recorded rather than papered over.** (i) `read_canonical` drops quarantined symbol-days and truncates history before a 5× single-day break (ruling 30), so a name whose panel ends mid-month for either of those reasons is booked as a delisting it did not suffer — the exit price is then the last *pre-break* close, which is conservative in level but wrong in kind. (ii) The panel is read only over `[start, end]`, so a name whose real history continues past `end` but whose last in-window close falls inside the final month's hold books a spurious exit; the mitigation is contractual, not algorithmic — the calibration driver passes `end` one month past the last month it uses and cuts the series (Task 6). Cost if wrong: (i) inflates measured spreads on names the vendor merely disagreed about, in the same direction as the real fix, which is exactly the direction that is hard to detect — the counter is the quarantine rate already reported per year (ruling 29) and the fact that the exit is capped at a real printed close, never at zero; (ii) is bounded to one month of a 36–47 month window and is eliminated outright by the driver's `end` offset.

    **Scope.** Calibration metrics only. `backtest/engine.py`'s forced-liquidation rule is a separate, ledgered decision for phase 1 and was not touched in this round, so the engine and the replication metric now book a delisting differently — an intentional, recorded divergence, not drift.

43. **A one-day spike is dropped, and of the breaks that remain a break on the symbol's final row through `end` is unconfirmed and drops that row rather than truncating.** Task 5 of the phase-1 hardening plan, amended in review before landing. Ledger events `e3e91597f8d143cd8954dbe9dff2fd0b` (the first, final-row-only form) and `5f8f7a7054bd46928cde766e33507afa` (`amends` the first; the rule as shipped), both `decision.reconcile.break_confirmation`, `max_jump = 5.0`. This changes a measured number: `read_canonical` is the only close series the universe screen, the replication metrics and the backtester may consume, so every read over a symbol with a junk print now answers differently. Why (report §9 gap 5): ruling 30's detector answers a break by dropping everything *before* it, so a single junk print — a 10× tick both vendors agree on, a partial back-adjustment landing in one night's batch — made that one row the entire series for the name at that `asof`; `universe.build`'s median close was then the junk price, and momentum's `near` close was the junk price. The rule as shipped, in two passes over the frame the caller has already bounded by `end`:

    > **(1) Spikes.** A row whose own ratio to the previous close is a break, and whose *next* close's ratio **to that same previous close** is inside `[1/max_jump, max_jump]`, is a spike: the level left and came straight back, and only one row can be responsible. Spike rows are dropped and the ratios recomputed across the gap. Null, non-finite and non-positive neighbours never qualify — prev, self and next all face the usability guard — so a break on the last row has no successor, is not a spike, and falls to (2).

    > **(2) Breaks in what remains.** A break on the symbol's final row through `end` is unconfirmed and that row is dropped, leaving the history intact. A break with at least one later row is confirmed and truncates everything before it, exactly as ruling 30 had it.

    Measuring the successor against the close *before* the break, rather than against the break itself, is the whole discrimination and the only load-bearing choice in (1): `[100, 1000, 800]` leaves the successor eight times where the series came from, so the level moved and stayed moved — a splice, kept and truncated as before — while `[102, 1020, 102]` returns to where it started. One row of evidence cannot establish a new regime; two can. Point-in-time is preserved because both passes are made on the frame through `end`, which the point-in-time consumers (`universe.build`, the fundamental signals) pass as their `asof` horizon — nothing after `end` is read, and the same spike is dropped for the *other* reason (unconfirmed final row) at the horizon where its revert is not yet visible. *Qualification (fix wave, decision D12):* two consumers do **not** pass `end` as their horizon — `metrics.monthly_longshort` and `engine._market_frame` read one panel through the window's end, so a confirmed break truncates history for every formation date in the window, including those before the break. Pre-existing since ruling 30, direction flatters, exposure a two-vendor-agreed 5× break on a name past the screens; registered in report §12.6 and fixed in the search branch's first prerequisite task (read with `max_jump=None`, cut per formation month, re-run to quantify).

    **Why the amendment was needed, recorded because the first form shipped through TDD and still had it.** With the final-row rule alone, `[100, 101, 102, 1020, 102]` at `end` = the last row returns **`[1020.0]`**: the reverting 102 is itself an unconfirmed final-row break and is dropped, which promotes the junk print to a *confirmed* break and truncates everything before it. The old code returned `[102.0]`. So the first form made the exact outcome the ruling exists to prevent *more* likely on the commonest shape of junk print, and it did so in a case none of the six new tests covered. Under the shipped rule that series gives `[100, 101, 102, 102]`, and `[100, 101, 102, 1020, 102, 103]` gives `[100, 101, 102, 102, 103]` — so a two-vendor-confirmed one-day spike strictly inside the window **is** now addressed, superseding the "not addressed here, and not yet observed" parenthetical the brief's draft of this ruling carried.

    **Cost if wrong, in three named cases.** (a) A genuine reverse split on the last day costs one day of that name's close; it is back the next session as a confirmed break. (b) A genuine two-day round trip in the underlying — a real 10× move that really does come back the next session — is read as a spike and its true close is dropped. At `max_jump = 5` that is a 5× move and a 5× retracement in two sessions on a name already past the $5 and $1M ADV screens; not observed, and the alternative is admitting the junk print it is indistinguishable from. (c) An **alternating** junk series — `[102, 1020, 102, 1020, 102]` — collapses to its first row: every interior row is a spike under the symmetric rule and the final row is an unconfirmed break. Not observed, documented here rather than special-cased, and it fails in the safe direction (too few rows, never a junk price).

    **Consequence for ruling 39, named rather than discovered later.** `metrics.monthly_longshort` prices a delisting exit at the name's **last canonical close**. A symbol whose last row that will ever exist is a real break — a reverse split printed on the final trading day before the delisting — never receives a confirming row, so that row is now dropped permanently and the exit is priced at the **pre-split** close. On a reverse split that is a *lower* price, which can fall under `DELIST_PRICE_FLOOR` ($1.0) and take the Shumway −30% haircut on top. Accepted as conservative — the haircut biases the measured spread down, and the pre-split close is a price two vendors printed while the post-split one is a price nothing will ever corroborate — and recorded so that a later reader does not read it as drift. It is also the one case where "tomorrow it is confirmed" is false.

45. **The engine carries a rename, holds a name through a gap of up to `MAX_GAP_DAYS` (5) trading days at its last vetted close, and exits on a merger or a longer gap at ruling 39's price.** Task 8 of the phase-1 hardening plan; ledger event `32163627a0424b8495909f85d3681071` (`decision.engine.delisting`: `max_gap_days = 5`, `delist_return = -0.30`, `delist_price_floor = 1.0`). This changes what `backtest/engine.py` books for every held name with a hole in its canonical series, so no phase-0 engine result is comparable with a phase-1 one; the calibration metrics of ruling 39 are untouched, and the engine now prices a delisting exit as they do. The three rules, verbatim:

    > **Rename.** On the first trading day `t` on which a held symbol `S` has **no vetted close**, a name change `(old=S, new=S′)` with `process_date ≤ t` that has not yet been applied and whose two symbols the **same-issuer test** does not separate carries the position into `S′`: shares, open tax lots, the pending target and the last mark move unchanged; nothing is traded, charged or realised. The same-issuer test takes the last ≤ 6 sessions strictly before `process_date` on which both symbols have a vetted close and refuses the rename when any pair of their log returns differs by more than `RENAME_DRIFT` (0.0025); fewer than two such sessions is no evidence, and no evidence carries. A symbol that is still printing is never carried, and neither is one whose target was moving as a different company before the rename — vendor histories are keyed by lineage, so a recycled ticker's bars belong to another issuer. Applied before the gap check, so a rename day is not a gap.
    >
    > **Gap tolerance.** A held symbol with no vetted close on `t` is held, marked at its last vetted close, for up to `MAX_GAP_DAYS` (5) consecutive trading days. On its return the position simply continues. The phase-0 rule — any hole is a delisting — would force a taxable round trip on every quarantined vendor disagreement, and the measured rate is 2.4% of bars.
    >
    > **Exit.** The position is exited when (a) a merger event for `S` with `process_date ≤ t` exists — at `cash_rate` per share for a cash merger, else at the last vetted close (share conversion is not modelled; recorded) — or (b) the gap exceeds `MAX_GAP_DAYS`, at the last vetted close, multiplied by `1 + DELIST_RETURN` when that close is below `DELIST_PRICE_FLOOR` (ruling 39's Shumway rule). The sale is dated at the last vetted close (the phase-0 convention: earlier tax year, shorter holding period — the conservative side of both), transaction costs are charged, and the event says which rule fired.
    >
    > *Cost if wrong:* a genuinely dead name is carried for five extra days at a stale mark (bounded: it is then exited exactly as before); a cash-merger `rate` from the vendor that is wrong prices the exit wrong — the counter is that the event carries both the rate and the last close. Engine and `metrics.monthly_longshort` now agree on the delisting exit price and haircut; they still differ on *when* (the engine on discovery + 5, the metric at the hold end), which is inherent to a daily engine and a monthly metric.

    **How "not yet applied" and "for `S`" are decided, because both are load-bearing.** A merger counts only when its `process_date` lies strictly after the position's last vetted close under the old name and on or before `t`; a rename counts only when its `process_date` lies **on or after** that close and on or before `t`, **and** the old symbol has no vetted close on `t`, **and** the two symbols' returns agree over their last common sessions before `process_date` — all three conjuncts as amended below. The position's own history is the point-in-time record of what it has lived through, and the merger table is keyed by a ticker that can be recycled, so a deal dated before the entry belongs to whatever `S` was then (`test_a_merger_before_the_entry_is_not_an_exit` pins this with a stale deal and a two-day hole after the entry — a name with no hole never reaches the merger check, so the brief's "keeps printing" scenario alone would not tell the two filters apart). A merger is examined only on a day the name has no vetted close, and the lower bound is permanent: an acquiree that prints on or after its `process_date` can never be priced at the deal — its later hole is a `gap_exceeded` exit at the last close, five days on. That is the right reading only if Alpaca's `process_date` is the first day the acquiree does not trade; if it turns out to be the last trading day, cash mergers are booked as gap exits at the last close, and the counter is `reason` on the event, which makes the split countable. The first rule to fire wins: a merger dated more than `MAX_GAP_DAYS` trading days after the name's last print is booked as `gap_exceeded` at the last close, because the gap rule exits the position before the deal is ever examined — not drift. A name-change row with `old_symbol == new_symbol` (Alpaca's company-name changes; 211 rows in the table) is not a rename and is ignored, and a cash merger whose `cash_rate` is missing or non-positive is priced at the last close. Two same-day merger rows for one name (Alpaca can report one deal in more than one bucket) resolve to the alphabetically first `kind`, i.e. `cash` when present — recorded, not resolved. Event shape: `engine.rename {strategy, symbol, new_symbol, ts, process_date, qty}`; `engine.forced_liquidation` gains `last_close`, `reason ∈ {merger_cash, merger_stock, merger_stock_and_cash, gap_exceeded}` and `gap_days`.

    **Tests re-pointed rather than deleted.** `test_delisted_holding_is_liquidated_at_its_last_close` now expects discovery on day 60 + `MAX_GAP_DAYS` with `reason = gap_exceeded`, `gap_days = 6` and a flat equity tail from day 60 — the gap is marked at the last close, then cash, then B; `test_quarantine_gap_forces_a_liquidation`, whose docstring documented the edge this ruling closes, is replaced by `test_a_short_gap_is_held_through_not_liquidated` (a month-end falls inside its gap and the rebalance targets B while A is unquoted; the B buy is skipped because the entry left no cash — deterministic, five runs); `test_forced_liquidation_is_taxed_in_the_year_of_the_last_close` computes its discovery date as last close + 1 + `MAX_GAP_DAYS`, its tax-year assertion unchanged. The brief's mutation check (c) — dropping the sorted merge in `TaxLots.rename` — survived the brief's own test, because with those values both orderings consume the same two long-term lots; `test_rename_merges_by_purchase_date_so_fifo_stays_oldest_first`, where the pre-existing `NEW` lot is the youngest, is the test that kills it.

    **One deviation from the brief's code, recorded.** Where `S′` is already held — reachable only if the vendor panel carries both names on overlapping days — the brief's rename overwrote `S′`'s last mark and pending target with `S`'s; as shipped the fresher mark is kept and the targets add, as the shares and the lots already did.     **Amended after PR #5's external review (three rounds, final form).** A rename `old → new` dated `process_date` is applied on the first day `t ≥ process_date` on which **(i)** the old symbol has no vetted close, **(ii)** the position's last vetted close is on or before `process_date`, and **(iii)** the two series were moving together beforehand: the last ≤ `RENAME_OVERLAP` (6) sessions strictly before `process_date` on which both symbols have a vetted close are compared on log returns, and the rename is refused when any pair differs by more than `RENAME_DRIFT` (0.0025); fewer than two such sessions is no evidence, and no evidence carries. **Why each.** (i) The store is keyed by a symbol's *current* owner, so on a recycled ticker the bars under `old` are another issuer's and carrying the position moves it into an unrelated price series without a trade — 100 of the 2,864 renames have that shape, 75 on Alpaca alone, `IR→TT` at roughly four times the price. (ii) Was `<`; under the vendor's possible "last trading day" semantics the old symbol prints *on* its `process_date` and vanishes the next session, and a strict bound would leave the rename permanently undue and gap the name out. (iii) The quote guard alone still mis-carries a recycled ticker on any day the old series happens to have a hole, and the quarantine rate is 2.4% of bars. **Threshold provenance:** measured on the canonical panel over the 210 renames the engine could be holding through — same-issuer pairs differ by at most 0.00064, recycled tickers by at least 0.0127, no overlap on either Alpaca or the canonical panel, so 0.0025 separates them with nothing near it. Log returns and not price levels, because a split around the rename re-bases the surviving series (`ECA→OVV`) and the levels then differ by the split ratio for two series that are the same company. Point-in-time: only sessions strictly before `process_date` are read, from the canonical frame *before* `_market_frame` truncates it at the run's start, so a rename in the run's opening sessions is judged on the warm-up window instead of falling through to "no evidence". **The gate this replaced, recorded because it was measured:** requiring the new symbol's series not to predate the rename by more than 30 days is plausible and wrong — the nightly ingests a rename's target and the re-base job pulls its history whole, so a genuine rename's target usually starts long before it. It blocks 45 of the 69 genuine ticker changes (`PPDF→FINV` 2019-11-29 and `DGSE→ELA` 2019-12-18, both inside the development window) to catch 66 of the 75 fictitious ones; the returns test costs none of them. **Registered residual:** a recycled ticker whose new symbol has no pre-rename overlap with the old one — 66 of the 75 shapes on the canonical panel — has no evidence to judge and is protected by the quote guard alone, so a missing vetted close on the session the rename comes due carries the position into the other issuer's series: ≈1.6 expected occurrences warehouse-wide over 2016–2026 at the 2.4% quarantine rate, ≈0 in the development window, whose only such shape (`BBT→TFC` 2019-12-09) the returns test separates anyway. Every occurrence is visible as an `engine.rename` event. Pinned by `test_a_rename_is_not_applied_while_the_old_symbol_keeps_printing` (i), `test_a_rename_dated_on_the_last_printed_day_still_carries` (ii), `test_a_recycled_ticker_is_never_carried_even_across_a_hole` and `test_a_genuine_rename_whose_new_history_was_backfilled_is_carried` (iii), `test_a_rename_early_in_the_run_is_judged_on_the_warm_up_window` (the warm-up read) and `test_a_rename_with_too_little_overlap_to_judge_is_carried` (the no-evidence default); every pre-existing rename test is unchanged.
